## Day 1–2 Lab Description

Day 1 and Day 2 of the Generative AI for Business course focused on understanding the basic concepts of Artificial Intelligence and its practical use in aviation management. In Day 1, I explored ChatGPT and Google Gemini and compared their responses to the same aviation-related prompt. The activity helped me understand how AI tools generate information and why their responses should be checked before being used. I also learned the difference between retrieved information and generated information. Retrieved information comes from an existing source such as an official airline system or policy, while generated information is newly created by an AI tool. The lab also showed the importance of protecting sensitive information such as passenger details, booking references and payment information.

Day 2 focused on the difference between Traditional AI and Generative AI. Traditional AI is mainly used for tasks such as prediction, classification, detection, recommendation and forecasting. For example, it can predict flight delays or forecast passenger demand. Generative AI, on the other hand, is used to create new content such as flight-delay messages, customer-service responses, advertisements and presentation outlines. The lab also explained that both types of AI can work together in an aviation business workflow.

One important example was flight delay management. Traditional AI can analyse operational data and predict a possible delay. Generative AI can then use the verified information to prepare a passenger notification. However, a human employee must review the information, check company policies and approve the final communication.

Overall, Day 1 and Day 2 helped me understand how AI tools can support aviation-management tasks while showing the importance of verification, human judgement and responsible use of AI.
